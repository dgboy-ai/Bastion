### THE ANSWER: 7 Requirements for Gold

#### 1. SOLVE A REAL PROBLEM (Not a Toy)
**Source:** UGAHacks, Opportunity Hack, AWS Hackathon
- "Whether it is a product geared towards personal safety, personal improvement, fostering relationships, or cleaning up your local streets, a winning project will make a positive impact on your community"
- "Whether the team chose a real problem or a toy problem" - judges explicitly check this
- Must affect "a number of people in the community" - not just one person

#### 2. HELP GROUPS OF PEOPLE (Not Just One)
**Source:** Good Neighbor Track Requirements
- "helps groups of people, not just one — neighborhoods, nonprofits, food banks, schools, libraries, small local orgs"
- The problem must be shared by MULTIPLE people
- The solution must serve a COMMUNITY, not an individual

#### 3. WORKING DEMO DEPLOYED PUBLICLY
**Source:** Opportunity Hack Scorecard
- "Project is deployed somewhere public (not just running on the laptop)"
- "Error states are handled gracefully"
- "Common edge cases (empty state, slow network, invalid input) don't break the app"
- "Estimated work remaining to reach MVP is small"

#### 4. MEASURABLE IMPACT
**Source:** AWS Hackathon (20% weight), Social Impact Criteria
- "What real-world problem/process is it solving or improving?"
- "What (measurable) impact does it have?"
- Must be able to quantify: X people helped, Y hours saved, Z lives improved

#### 5. USE REQUIRED TECH MEANINGFULLY
**Source:** AWS Hackathon (50% weight - Technical Execution)
- Must use Strands Agents SDK (required)
- Must use AWS Bedrock (required)
- "Is the solution well-architected?"
- "Is it reproducible?"
- Agent must do real work, not just call an API

#### 6. CLEAR PITCH IN DEMO VIDEO
**Source:** AWS Hackathon (10% weight), Multiple sources
- "Does it include the end-to-end agentic workflow?"
- "How is the demo quality and clarity?"
- Must cover: (1) the problem you're solving, (2) who it's for, (3) why it matters
- Maximum 5 minutes

#### 7. SUSTAINABLE BEYOND HACKATHON
**Source:** Social Impact Criteria, Connect4Impact
- "Could this idea continue beyond the hackathon or scale to have lasting impact?"
- "Is the solution scalable, adaptable, or has potential for integration with existing systems?"
- README with clear setup, deploy, environment variables

### JUDGING CRITERIA BREAKDOWN (from Research)

| Criteria | Weight | What Judges Look For |
|----------|--------|---------------------|
| **Technical Execution** | 50% | Uses Strands SDK meaningfully, well-architected, reproducible |
| **Potential Value/Impact** | 20% | Real-world problem, measurable impact, specific audience |
| **Creativity** | 10% | Novelty of problem, novelty of approach |
| **Functionality** | 10% | Agent works as expected, scalable |
| **Demo Presentation** | 10% | End-to-end workflow, clear pitch, quality demo |

### WHAT SEPARATES GOLD FROM SILVER

**Silver projects:**
- Solve a problem for ONE person
- Have a demo that runs locally only
- Use the required tech as a checkbox
- Have a vague "impact" claim

**Gold projects:**
- Solve a problem for MANY people
- Have a demo deployed publicly
- Use the required tech as the CORE of the solution
- Have SPECIFIC, QUANTIFIABLE impact

**Grand Prize projects:**
- All of the above PLUS
- Solve a problem that makes judges say "oh I've faced this"
- Have a pitch that's emotionally compelling
- Show the agent doing something NO OTHER solution does

---

### KEY INSIGHT FROM 12 YEARS OF DATA

From Opportunity Hack's analysis of 12 years of hackathons:

> "The most beautiful hackathon prototype is worthless to a nonprofit that can't deploy or maintain it."

**Translation:** It's not about the flashiest demo. It's about building something that ACTUALLY WORKS and COULD ACTUALLY BE USED.
